Footwear · FEA

Scarpa Vapor V · Finite Element Analysis

Rebuilt the climbing shoe in Rhinoceros, selected layered materials, and simulated multiple load cases in Abaqus to balance stiffness and sensitivity for advanced climbers.

Role
Computational design engineer
Tools
Rhino 6 · Abaqus CAE · Grasshopper
Timeline
4 weeks · solo study
Deliverables
Material report · FEA movies

Background

Rock climbing shoes are the interface between climbers and rock; the wrong fit or stiffness can hold you back. They typically have little padding and use a stiff midsole sandwiched between footbed and outsole to tune sensitivity. Vapor V needed better balance between edging support and smearing comfort.

  • 3D modeling in Rhinoceros 6
  • Material selection and layering study
  • FEA runs performed in Abaqus CAE
  • Biomechanical study and considerations

Digital reconstruction workflow

The legacy article walked through a meticulous rebuild of the shoe starting from taped foot forms. I recreated that process with updated captures, lofting the last, toe rand, and closure system in Rhino before porting them to Grasshopper where every layer became parametric.

  • Digitized the last, straps, and split sole with sub-millimeter tolerance.
  • Set live controls for rubber thickness, perforation density, and tensioning straps.
  • Kept the same narrative screenshots from the legacy page so readers can follow each modeling hop.

Material stack & instrumentation

The FEA studies only make sense if the materials act like their physical counterparts. I brought back the laminated diagrams from the previous site, annotated them with durometer and modulus values, and mapped every layer to temperature-dependent material cards.

  • Captured five rubber compounds and two textiles with uniaxial test data.
  • Inserted ventilation, tension straps, and the toe patch as discrete contact bodies.
  • Added instrumentation windows for strain gauges, mirroring the original documentation.

Load cases & solving strategy

The legacy workflow alternated text and imagery to explain the different boundary conditions. I restored that approach by splitting the solving strategy into toe-hook, smear, heel-tension, and twist families with callouts that link directly to their plots.

  • Toe and smear loads: 1.5-2.0 kN applied at the big toe and medial forefoot.
  • Heel tension: strap tightening plus opposing shear on the heel cup.
  • Twist: counter-rotating forefoot/heel to stress perforation patterns.

Outcomes

The study delivered actionable recommendations for Scarpa's future iterations, demonstrating where to reduce stiffness, how perforation patterns influence energy return, and which materials best balance comfort and control. It also produced a reusable Grasshopper definition to test additional concepts quickly.

  • Identified a midsole perforation pattern that reduces peak stress by 12% while keeping edging support.
  • Built a rendering + GIF library for stakeholders to compare options.
  • Documented a clear handoff for manufacturing partners.
